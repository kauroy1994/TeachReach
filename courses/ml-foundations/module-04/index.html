Statistical Learning, Estimation, and Generalization

Ava’s fictional exam score was 60. Module 03 adjusted the prediction from 55 to 55.70. Would the updated rule also help on the next student? To answer, we need to decide whose scores matter and which scores we may use while choosing the rule.

Where this lesson begins

Module 01 supplied the inputs and a prediction rule. Module 02 attached uncertainty to the prediction. Module 03 used a derivative to reduce the loss on one observed score. Here we ask what one observation can tell us about future errors.

Revisit Module 03

Learning outcomes

Population versus sample, hypothesis classes, expected risk, empirical risk, estimation, inductive bias, train/test distinction, and generalization.

  • Distinguish population objectives from empirical objectives.
  • Describe sampling variation and assumptions of a hypothesis class.
  • Recognize data leakage, inductive bias, and poor generalization.

End-of-module exercises

Question 1

A constant prediction of 12 meets observations 10, 11, 15. Write and compute its empirical squared risk.

Hint

Subtract each observation, square, then average.

Reveal the worked answer

R̂(c) = [(c−10)²+(c−11)²+(c−15)²]/3. At c=12 the value is (4+1+9)/3 = 14/3 squared units.

Question 2

A rule loses 1 on ordinary cases and 9 on unusual cases. Future cases are 75% ordinary. Find expected loss and compare with a sample containing one of each.

Hint

Weight by how often each case occurs.

Reveal the worked answer

Population risk is 0.75×1+0.25×9=3. The balanced sample gives (1+9)/2=5 because unusual cases have a larger share in that sample.

Question 3

Two candidate rules predict 70 on every training record. At a new input they predict 65 and 85. What evidence would discriminate between them?

Hint

Look for a record on which their predictions differ.

Reveal the worked answer

A labeled case on which they differ lets us compare losses. More such independently sampled cases better represent which inputs matter. Agreement on training records alone cannot break the tie.

Question 4

You fit twenty models and report the smallest of twenty test errors on the same students. Redesign the evaluation.

Hint

Which set made the selection?

Reveal the worked answer

The so-called test set selected the winner. Fit on training data, choose among twenty using validation data, then evaluate that single choice on untouched students sampled for the prediction goal.

Question 5

A score predictor uses a retake invitation sent after grading. Its held-out error is tiny. What is the first check?

Hint

Write down the time at which the prediction is needed.

Reveal the worked answer

If the goal is a prediction before the exam, the invitation arrives too late. Remove it, rebuild training features as of prediction time, and rerun the evaluation.

Question 6

Two independent small samples give estimates 12 and 18. Does the difference alone show that one estimate was computed incorrectly?

Hint

What changes between the averages?

Reveal the worked answer

No. Different sampled cases may contribute different individual losses. Check the arithmetic, sample selection and variation before attributing the difference to a mistake.
