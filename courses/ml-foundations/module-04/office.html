What does generalization mean for Ava’s example?

A prediction rule generalizes well when it has low loss on the future records we care about. Lowering Ava’s loss proves that her prediction improved. Predicting for new students also requires evidence about those students, the time of prediction and which examples were excluded from fitting.

Why learn from a sample if it can give the wrong answer?

Measuring everyone is often impossible. A sample drawn to represent the target population can estimate average loss and help select a rule. Examining how much the estimate changes across samples tells us about its precision. Selective sampling can still distort it.

Is the smallest training error always the best choice?

It identifies the best fit to those measurements under the chosen loss. A flexible rule can memorize observed values yet predict poorly elsewhere. Validation compares choices on fresh cases, and a separate test estimates the chosen procedure’s performance.
