Can points form groups without labels?

Four points sit at 1, 2, 8 and 9 on a line. Start centers at 1 and 8. The nearest-center groups are {1, 2} and {8, 9}. Their new means are 1.5 and 8.5.

WHAT YOU'LL LEARN

Module learning outcomes

  • Run and interpret k-means.
  • Explain PCA as maximizing retained variance.
  • Contrast hard clusters with probabilistic assignments.
LEARNING SEQUENCE

How this lesson builds

  1. Assign each point to its nearer center. Average each group and repeat. These are the two operations in k-means.
  2. Draw the same points with two measured features. Subtract each column mean. When two centered features rise together, their products tend to be positive; averaging the products measures covariance.
  3. Project points onto a horizontal, vertical, then tilted line by dropping perpendiculars. Identify which line preserves the greatest spread. Name that direction the first principal component before introducing eigenvectors.
  4. Compare assigning one cluster label with assigning probabilities of membership in several Gaussian groups. Explain what information a projection or a hard assignment loses.

MODULE 07 · PRACTICE AND IN-CLASS ASSESSMENT

What exercises will assess

Try it on paper

Four points sit at 1, 2, 8 and 9 on a line. Start centers at 1 and 8. The nearest-center groups are {1, 2} and {8, 9}. Their new means are 1.5 and 8.5.

For points 2, 4, 10 and centers 2, 10, where does the first center move after one round?

Hint

List the points closer to 2, then average them.

Reveal answerHide answer

2 and 4 are nearer the first center. Their mean is 3. The second center stays at 10.
