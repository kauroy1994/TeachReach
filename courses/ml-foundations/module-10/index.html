WHAT YOU'LL LEARN

Learning outcomes

  • Describe a self-supervised objective and the information it uses.
  • Implement a simple contrastive or reconstruction-based task.
  • Evaluate embeddings with similarity and downstream probes.
HOW THIS MODULE IS TAUGHT

How this lesson builds

  1. Mask one part of an example and score a reconstruction against the original. Identify exactly where the target came from.
  2. Create two crops of the same image. Treat those as a positive pair and compare them with a crop of a different image. Discuss which changes should preserve identity for this task.
  3. Represent each crop by a short vector, called an embedding. Calculate distances for a positive and a negative pair before writing a loss that rewards the intended ordering.
  4. Train on the input-derived task, then freeze the representation and evaluate it on a small labeled task. Check an example where two different images show the same class.
Every module includes practice exercises.
The graded variant is administered separately in class, at the end of Module 10.

Try it on paper

Cover the middle entry in [2, 4, 6]. The original entry, 4, supplies a target for predicting the hidden value. Creating targets from the input is one form of self-supervised learning.

An anchor has coordinate 2. Its positive partner is at 3 and a negative partner is at 2.5. Which partner is currently closer? What should the pair-training objective encourage?

Hint

Compute both absolute distances.

Reveal answerHide answer

The negative is closer (0.5 versus 1). The objective should encourage the positive partner to be closer relative to the negative.

Learning skills assessed

  • Construct a contrastive objective for given examples.
  • Interpret embeddings and a failure due to poor negatives.
