STAGE 3 OUTCOMES

By the end of this stage, you can…

  • Explain representation learning and train deeper neural networks.
  • Construct self-supervised objectives and interpret embeddings.
  • Implement attention and a small transformer with sequence data.
  • Explain autoregressive language modeling, tokenization, and adaptation.
  • Compare the mechanisms behind VAEs, GANs, and diffusion.
Stage milestone: Understand modern architectures by deriving and implementing their core mechanisms.
ASSESSMENT THROUGHOUT THE STAGE

Learn, practice, demonstrate.

  1. Read each module's worked explanation and complete its interactive exercises.
  2. Use feedback and the module notebook to understand why the methods work.
  3. At the end of every module, complete a new in-class variant of the exercises administered and graded by the instructor.

Published practice is not the graded variant. Assessment topics and methods are listed on each module page.

MODULE 09 · STAGE 3

Deep Learning and Representation Learning

Learning useful intermediate features

Syllabus preview

Depth, feature hierarchies, convolutional intuition, residual connections, normalization, optimization choices, and learned representations.

Learning outcomes

  • Explain how depth changes the functions a model can express.
  • Implement a small deep network and inspect intermediate features.
  • Recognize vanishing gradients and the purpose of residual connections.

Practice & in-class assessment

Module practice will target: Intermediate-feature visualization; Network-depth and residual-path experiment.

The instructor-administered, graded in-class variant at this module's end assesses equivalent skills:

  • Identify the role of a representation layer.
  • Analyze a deep-network optimization or shape problem.

MODULE 10 · STAGE 3

Self-Supervised Learning and Embeddings

Learning useful signals without hand-labeling everything

Syllabus preview

Pretext tasks, contrastive learning, positive/negative pairs, embedding spaces, similarity, reconstruction, and pretraining.

  • Describe a self-supervised objective and the information it uses.
  • Implement a simple contrastive or reconstruction-based task.

Practice & in-class assessment

Module practice will target: Embedding-space similarity explorer; Contrastive-pair experiment.

The instructor-administered, graded in-class variant at this module's end assesses equivalent skills:

  • Construct a contrastive objective for given examples.
  • Interpret embeddings and a failure due to poor negatives.

MODULE 11 · STAGE 3

Sequence Modeling, Attention, and Transformers

Learning relationships across a sequence

Syllabus preview

Sequence representations, positional information, queries/keys/values, dot-product attention, masking, multi-head attention, and transformer blocks.

Learning outcomes

  • Compute attention weights from small query/key matrices.
  • Explain causal masks and positional information.
  • Implement a minimal attention layer in PyTorch.

Practice & in-class assessment

Module practice will target: Attention-weight heatmap; Causal-mask and token-position experiment.

The instructor-administered, graded in-class variant at this module's end assesses equivalent skills:

  • Calculate scaled dot-product attention for small tensors.
  • Explain how a mask changes accessible context.

MODULE 12 · STAGE 3

Language Modeling and Large Language Models

From next-token probabilities to language behavior

Syllabus preview

Tokens and tokenization, autoregressive likelihood, cross-entropy, decoding and sampling, pretraining, adaptation, and evaluating language models.

Learning outcomes

  • Explain a next-token prediction objective.
  • Compare greedy, temperature, and top-k sampling.
  • Describe limitations of evaluation and the difference between training and adaptation.

Practice & in-class assessment

Module practice will target: Next-token distribution and sampling lab; Tiny language-model training walkthrough.

The instructor-administered, graded in-class variant at this module's end assesses equivalent skills:

  • Compute sequence log-likelihood or perplexity.
  • Contrast decoding choices on identical token probabilities.

MODULE 13 · STAGE 3

Generative Modeling: VAEs, GANs, and Diffusion

How models learn to generate new observations

Syllabus preview

Latent-variable models, variational inference intuition, encoder/decoder, adversarial objectives, diffusion noise processes, denoising, and sampling.

Learning outcomes

  • Contrast likelihood-based, adversarial, and denoising generation.
  • Describe the encoder–latent–decoder computation in a VAE.
  • Implement and interpret a simple noise-and-denoising sequence.

Practice & in-class assessment

Module practice will target: Latent-space sampling explorer; Progressive-noise and denoising visualization.

The instructor-administered, graded in-class variant at this module's end assesses equivalent skills:

  • Explain a VAE or GAN training objective at a conceptual/mathematical level.
  • Reason about diffusion steps and generation limitations.
