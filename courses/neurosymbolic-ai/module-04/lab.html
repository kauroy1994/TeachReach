Explore a case

Complete chain

The desk has recorded Requested(Ava, C1), Oriented(Ava), and Available(C1). R1 derives eligibility from orientation. R2 requires a request, eligibility and availability.

Build the shortest chain to Ready(Ava, C1) using these rules.

Reveal the result and reasoning

R1 uses Oriented(Ava) to derive Eligible(Ava). R2 combines that intermediate conclusion with Requested(Ava, C1) and Available(C1) to derive Ready(Ava, C1).

Wrong item

Replace Available(C1) with Available(C2) and keep the other records and rules from the complete chain.

Which step still works, and which premise prevents the next one?

Reveal the result and reasoning

R1 still derives Eligible(Ava). Ava requested C1, so R2 requires Available(C1). The availability record for C2 cannot supply it.

Independent evidence

Ava’s orientation record has been withdrawn. The desk separately verifies Eligible(Ava) directly. Requested(Ava, C1) and Available(C1) remain recorded.

Can the desk still justify ready? Which trace survives?

Reveal the result and reasoning

Yes. R2 can use the directly verified Eligible(Ava) with the request and availability. A trace through R1 has lost its orientation premise. The direct eligibility record gives a separate route.
