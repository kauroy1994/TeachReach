Explore a case

Quiet shift

Model A starts at 4 minutes, adds 6 when busy and subtracts 2 with an extra clerk. Keep the shift quiet.

What does the model predict when extra help is added?

Reveal the result and reasoning

It predicts 2 minutes with help versus 4 without, a change of −2 minutes. This comes from the specified rule.

Same observations, new rule

Model B starts at 4 minutes, adds 4 when busy and has no staffing term. The observed busy, extra-clerk shift waited 8 minutes.

What does Model B predict for that shift if the extra clerk is removed?

Reveal the result and reasoning

It still gives 8 minutes. Having observed 8 with extra help does not distinguish Model B from Model A.

A different observation

The desk now reports a busy shift with usual staffing and an 11-minute wait. The two toy rules ignore variation between equally busy shifts.

Does this match either model’s exact prediction for that input?

Reveal the result and reasoning

Neither. Model A predicts 10 and B predicts 8. A is closer on this case. A single mismatch still calls for revisiting the rules or measuring other sources of variation.
