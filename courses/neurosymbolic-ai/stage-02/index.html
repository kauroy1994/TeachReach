Reading and extension

DeepProbLog: Neural Probabilistic Logic Programming

Read the handwritten-digit addition example and Appendix A, Listing 1. A neural predicate assigns probabilities to the possible digits in an image. An ordinary addition rule then relates those uncertain digit readings to an observed sum. Training can use the sum as a target even when the individual digits are unlabeled.

Robin Manhaeve, Sebastijan Dumančić, Angelika Kimmig, Thomas Demeester and Luc De Raedt (2018) · Open the reading ↗

Connect the reading to your model

Try a classroom version in which each picture is either 1 or 2. The first picture gives probabilities 0.60 and 0.40 for those digits. The second gives 0.25 and 0.75. Assume the two readings are independent. What probability does the addition rule give the sum 3? What information would be lost by keeping just the most likely digit in each picture?

Reveal a worked answer

Two mutually exclusive pairs make 3. Reading 1 then 2 contributes 0.60 × 0.75 = 0.45. Reading 2 then 1 contributes 0.40 × 0.25 = 0.10. Their total is 0.55. Keeping only 1 and 2 gives a single answer of 3 and discards the alternative explanations and their weights. In the desk exercise, turning a learned score into a yes-or-no fact before applying a rule makes a similar choice about what evidence to retain.
