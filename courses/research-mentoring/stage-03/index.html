CS 696 · Stage 03 of 04

Test a simulator under change

Two programs can reproduce the same recorded process and disagree when a condition changes. Design a test that tells them apart.

Bring your work from Stage 02 · a process model and a paired comparison.

Module 05

When a changed queue exposes a wrong answer

Construct a what-if question that distinguishes two competing scheduling rules, calculate its expected answer independently, and use an event trace to locate the first discrepancy.

Bring a frozen original answer, the one rule it may have learned incorrectly, two hand-calculated schedules, a control and the shortest trace that exhibits the first impossible event.

Module 06

Compare accuracy, coverage and cost on changed queues

Compare two existing reporting policies on the same what-if questions. Count incorrect answers shown, correct answers withheld and work performed. Defend a conclusion whose limits you can state.

Bring both archived response logs, coverage and accuracy with denominators, work calculations and a short recommendation. Identify a changed queue whose independent record would give you evidence beyond these four families.

Reading for the meeting

Robert G. Sargent · Validation and Verification of Simulation Models

Choose one check of the implementation and one check of the model’s intended use. What evidence could pass the first and fail the second?

Before you move on

Carry a claim from the comparison, the specific supporting records, the alternative explanation and the evidence still needed into Module 07. There you will check what a new set of records actually establishes. Keep each claim attached to the observation or rule that could support or overturn it.
