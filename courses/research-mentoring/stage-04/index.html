CS 696 · Stage 04 of 04

Reason from agent trajectories

Annotate what happened in a simulated episode. Use an explicit transition model to ask what could happen under another action, then defend the limits of the answer.

Bring your work from Stage 03 · a counterexample and an evaluation of when to trust an answer.

Module 07

Read an event from a simulated trajectory

Annotate a virtual cart’s zone transitions and use the annotations to answer questions about its past, make conditional predictions, and identify futures the available evidence leaves open.

Bring the two visible prefixes, an event definition, the four-item answer sheet and a suffix-swap test. Include the observed ticks and assumptions beside each answer.

Module 08

Defend an evidence-grounded result

Reproduce an event-QA score from frozen records, state its scope, and defend the assumptions behind a model-based what-if answer.

Provide a one-page memo, a handoff bundle with visible prefixes, human-readable questions, reference and candidate answers, transition rule, manifest and scorer, and a brief oral defense. Have another reader run the command and note their interpreter version, output and any clarification required.

Reading for the meeting

Poole and Mackworth · Representing States, Actions, and Goals

Write the precondition and effect of one action. Which part of the resulting state can you verify from an observation?

Before you move on

A concrete next study asks whether requiring both adjacent observations reduces unsupported entry claims on unseen runs with occasional missing ticks. Freeze the event definition and both answer policies. Reserve whole episode groups, keep suffixes hidden, and have independent readers label answerability and acceptable evidence before scoring. Report unsupported claims together with erroneous abstentions. Return to the mentoring meeting with the blinded case inventory and the first disagreement to adjudicate.
