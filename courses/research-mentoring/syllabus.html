CS 696 · 3 credit hours · Fall 2026

Independent study syllabus

A one-to-one graduate research course for a beginning doctoral student with a bachelor’s degree in computer science.

Mentor Kaushik Roy · Doctoral mentee Ashraf Abdul-Muumin

Where to start

Bring familiarity with functions, loops, dictionaries, averages and reading short Python programs. Module 01 introduces the notation for linking a claim to evidence. Later modules introduce process simulation, experimental controls and state transitions as you need them. Every notebook uses Python’s standard library and includes the small dataset it needs.

What you will be able to do

  • Represent a versioned filing requirement, cite the text that authorizes a mapping, and explain when an ambiguity needs human judgment.
  • Build an executable process model from source clauses and compare it with a retrieval baseline on the same questions.
  • Design a controlled change that exposes a simulator error, and quantify the tradeoff between answering, withholding an answer, and the cost of checking.
  • Distinguish observations from model-dependent predictions in a trajectory and defend an independently reproducible claim about an agent’s behavior.

How to use a mentoring meeting

Before the meeting, write down a prediction, run the example and keep the output that surprised you. Bring the module’s requested research artifact and one unresolved decision.

At the meeting, show a case that supports your interpretation and one that challenges it. Explain what you would change if your mentor disagreed. Afterwards, record the decision, its rationale and the next check in your research notebook.

Stages and research artifacts

Stage 01

Represent rules and their sources

Trace a reuse decision through two versions of a regulatory filing. Build a representation that lets a reviewer inspect the decision and its source.

  1. 01 · Frame a document-reuse question
  2. 02 · Decide which moves the evidence allows

Bring forward An evidence table and a versioned mapping

Stage 02

Answer questions with executable models

Turn prose about a workflow into a simulation. Compare the answers you can retrieve from a document with the answers that require executing its rules.

  1. 03 · Turn a process description into a what-if tool
  2. 04 · Compare lookup and execution on matched interventions

Bring forward A process model and a paired comparison

Stage 03

Test a simulator under change

Two programs can reproduce the same recorded process and disagree when a condition changes. Design a test that tells them apart.

  1. 05 · When a changed queue exposes a wrong answer
  2. 06 · Compare accuracy, coverage and cost on changed queues

Bring forward A counterexample and an evaluation of when to trust an answer

Stage 04

Reason from agent trajectories

Annotate what happened in a simulated episode. Use an explicit transition model to ask what could happen under another action, then defend the limits of the answer.

  1. 07 · Read an event from a simulated trajectory
  2. 08 · Defend an evidence-grounded result

Bring forward An annotated trajectory, reproducible result and oral defense
